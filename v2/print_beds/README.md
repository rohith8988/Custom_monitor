**The aluminium spine is not printed.** 160 × 130 × 3 mm cut from sheet, four

the VESA mount.

## Six pieces, not seven

v1.2 needed a `frame_key` to hold the front-frame butt joint together. v2's
frame seams are **lap joints**, so the joint is its own key.

## The seams

Three seams, and no two of them are on the same line.

**Front frame — staggered, and lapped.** The top rail parts at **x = +45**, the
bottom rail at **x = −45**, and the step between them runs through the empty
bezel opening, so it costs nothing. At the visible face each seam is a clean
butt; behind z = 1.5 mm it overlaps:

| | lap | shelf | + butt | = bond | v1.2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| top rail | 14 mm | 101.5 mm² | 69.3 mm² | **170.8 mm², shear** | 64 mm², bare butt, **peel** |
| bottom rail | 20 mm | 55.0 mm² | 36.0 mm² | **91.0 mm², shear** | 36 mm², bare butt, **peel** |

Each half gets **one tongue and one notch** — the top tongue belongs to the
right half, the bottom tongue to the left — so they key into each other both
ways and the two halves come out the same size.

Why this matters: v1.2 cut both rails at x = 0. Two joints on one line have no
lever arm between them, which makes them a hinge rather than a joint, and the
bottom one had no key and no screw within 178 mm. 90 mm of stagger turns
relative rotation of the halves into a couple the shells actually resist.

**Rear panel — x = 0.** Spliced by the I/O block (y −95…−29), by the aluminium
spine (y −25…105), and by `rear_splice` bonded into its recess. The recess
stops at y = 106 so it does not break through the two seam-straddle screw
seats — v1.2 ran it full height and did.

## Assembly

1. Bond the two front-frame halves at the laps. Thin CA or slow epoxy, cure
   **flat on a table** — the laps self-align, let them.
2. Bond `rear_splice` into its recess across the rear-panel seam. Cure flat.
3. Spine: order it laser-cut from `../aluminium_spine/` (see its README), tap the
   4 × Ø3.3 holes M4 **through**, and countersink the eight Ø2.7 holes on the LCD side.
   Because the holes go through, the VESA screw must not stick out more than **5.5 mm**
   past the arm plate (M4 × 8 with the portable stand's plate = 4.4).
4. Epoxy the spine to the rear panel's inner face, located by the four printed
   pips, clamped by 8 × M2.5×8 csk from the panel side. Cure flat. The epoxy is
   the load path; the screws are clamps and shear pins.
5. Screw the driver board to the four 3 mm standoffs inside `io_block` (**M2.5×5** pan),
   block roof-down on the bench, board on top of the standoffs, ports facing up.
6. **1.5 mm foam tape on the three clamp ribs only** — top and both sides.
   Nothing on the cross-ribs or the spine: those are stiffeners held 2.0 mm off
   the panel on purpose, because AUO's handling precaution 8 says not to press
   the reflector.
7. Lay both front-frame halves face-down, drop the panel into the pocket and
   push it onto the **+X and +Y datum pads**. It is datumed, not centred — that
   is what keeps the bezel even.
8. Route eDP (panel → CN10) and backlight (CN11) through the bay opening.
9. Invert `io_block` onto the rear panel, locating ring into the groove,
   4 × M2.5×8 csk from inside.
10. Snap the rear panel's bottom edge under the frame's tongue, pivot down, fit
    the 17 perimeter screws.

## Screws — M2.5 × 8, except the board (M2.5 × 5)

| Where | Qty | Head | Into |
|---|---|---|---|
| perimeter: rear panel → frame | 17 | countersunk | Ø2.1 blind pilot |
| I/O block → rear panel | 4 | countersunk | Ø2.1 blind pilot |
| driver board → block standoffs | 4 | pan / cheese, **M2.5×5** | Ø2.1 blind pilot, 4.4 deep |
| spine → rear panel | 8 | countersunk | Ø2.1 blind pilot |
| **total** | **33** | | |
| VESA arm → spine | 4 | M4, per your arm | tapped aluminium, **5.5 mm max** past the arm plate |

Use **thread-forming screws for plastics** (Delta PT, Plastite) in the Ø2.1
pilots, not machine screws. You want to open this again to get the panel out,
and machine screws chew a plastic thread out in two or three cycles.

## Slicer

- **Elephant-foot compensation 0.15–0.20 mm.** Both visible faces are bed
  faces, so the first-layer bulge lands exactly on the seam line. Without it a
  squashed first layer holds every joint open by the amount it bulges.
- **No supports on any bed.** Unsupported area is 0.6–1.8 % of each part.
- Bridges, honestly: the frame's snap tongue is a **1.0 mm** bridge, the rear
  panel's tongue rebate **1.4 mm**, the block's vents 2.0–2.5 mm. The port
  openings bridge **their own width — up to 19.8 mm for the DisplayPort** — in
  the block's 2 mm wall. That is an ordinary bridge and PETG spans it with the
  fan on, but expect a little droop on the first layer over each port. It is
  inside the block and does not show.
- Consider **PETG-CF** for the rear panel: roughly double the modulus, much
  less warp on a 181 × 233 flat plate, and the matte speckled bed finish is a
  closer match to the reference photos than plain PETG's slight gloss.

## Before you print bed 5

`BRD_STACK` is assumed at 13.5 mm against a spec of 12.6 ± 0.3, and the board's
hole and connector positions were read off the dimensioned drawing, not quoted
from text. **Measure your actual XY_H2EDP_UHD95T_V3.1 before committing to this
print**, and check `EDP_BODY_W` / `EDP_BODY_D` / `EDP_MATED_T` on the panel —
those three set `REAR_GAP`, and they are estimates, not datasheet values.
